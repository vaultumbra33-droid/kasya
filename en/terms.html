2. What KASYA is

KASYA is an app for encrypted messaging and calls that connects phone to phone (P2P), with no central message server. It is based on Jami, a free software project. It is distributed as an APK file from our website, not through app stores.

3. Your account and your keys

  • Nobody, including us, can recover your password, your PIN or your keys. If you lose your phone, uninstall the app, or wipe it with the emergency key, you lose the account and its messages.
  • You decide whether to make backups and where to keep them. You are responsible for safeguarding your PIN and your exported files.

5. Security features and their limits

KASYA includes tools to protect your privacy. None of them offers absolute security; it is worth knowing their limits:

  • Photos without location: removes GPS and device and author data from the JPEG photos you send. Other formats and non-photo files are not modified.
  • Disappearing messages: each phone deletes its copy when the time is up. This depends on both people using KASYA and does not prevent screenshots or photos of the screen.
  • Access PIN: protects opening the app; it does not encrypt the rest of your phone.
  • Emergency key: erases all KASYA content without asking and cannot be undone. It does not delete files you exported outside the app.

6. Privacy

What we do not do

  • We have no user accounts, no message server, no advertising, and no third-party analytics or tracking.
  • We do not collect, sell or share your personal data: we do not have it.

Where your data lives

Your messages, contacts, photos and files live on your phone and on the phones of the people you talk to. When you export or back up, the resulting file contains your conversations without the app's protection: take care of it.

What does happen on a P2P network

  • To find and connect to each other, phones use a distributed network (OpenDHT) and supporting technical services (STUN/TURN) that may be operated by third parties. Those nodes can see your IP address and technical connection data, but not the content of your messages or calls, which are encrypted.
  • Your contacts see what you choose to share with them: your name, your photo, your location if you send it, and so on.
  • A public channel can be followed by anyone who has its link.
  • News: only when you open that tab, the app downloads each outlet's public RSS feed directly from its own servers, without going through one of ours. Those outlets can see your IP address, as on any website. Tapping a story opens it in your browser, where that outlet's policies apply. KASYA keeps the downloaded headlines on your phone for 7 days, only to show you earlier days.

Phone permissions

KASYA requests only what its features need: network, notifications, microphone and camera (calls and photos), files and, if you choose, location. You can review them in Android Settings > Apps > KASYA.
